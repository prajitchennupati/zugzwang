Escape Zugzwang

Sep 30, 2026

In chess, zugzwang is the moment when any move you make weakens your position, and the rules still insist that you make one. Most people meet this position well outside the sixty-four squares: in a company, a career, or a decision that can’t wait any longer.

This is the first of our updates. We’ll use these notes to share what we’re building, what we’re learning along the way, and the occasional position worth studying.

Why the name

Zugzwang is uncomfortable because standing still isn’t an option. We think that discomfort is useful. It forces clarity about what actually matters, which pieces are really defending something, and which squares you can afford to give up.

The way out of zugzwang is rarely a brilliant move. It’s usually a better position, built a few moves earlier.

Much of our work is about those earlier moves: seeing the squeeze before it arrives, keeping a spare tempo in reserve, and making sure that when you do have to move, you have somewhere good to go.

What’s next

We’ll write here when there’s something worth saying: progress, announcements, and ideas we’re working through. If there’s a position you’re stuck in, write to us at hello@zugzwg.com. We read everything.

Your move.
